Framework audit

Tiny Toybox Games · 563 modules, 614 tests · every number below re-measured directly, not taken from a survey

The short version

The framework itself is in better shape than the drift suggests. materialFactory (109 importers), the particle registry (42) and the disposal scope are genuinely shared and correctly used almost everywhere; several files carry written postmortems for the exact leak classes I went looking for, and had already fixed them.

What is weak is not the framework — it is the guards that are supposed to hold new code to it. Four of the five room guards enumerate the three existing rooms by hand, so a fourth room is graded by nothing. And the single most load-bearing geometric rule in the app has no home in src/ at all: it exists as eleven copies.

Verified findings

2. The framing predicate has no homereuse

"Is this thing properly on screen" is the rule the letterbox removal, the toybox framing, the opening turn and every camera solve all turn on. It is implemented eleven times — three in tests, eight in probes — with three different threshold pairs, and zero times in src/.

ThresholdsMeaningCopies
0.85 / 0.60reachable by turning5
0.85 / 0.90the room opened onto it4
0.70 / 0.85a toybox is a destination1

The differing thresholds are argued for, well, in three separate headers — that part is deliberate and right. What nobody argued for is copying the twelve-line projection algorithm to vary two constants. One copy already diverges: playroom-toybox-framing records "behind the camera" as a flag where the other two hard-fail.

noCopiedConstants.test.mjs exists precisely to catch this and is structurally blind here: its rule is "a constant src/ exports must be imported, not retyped", and these constants never enter src/. I confirmed there are no matches for CENTRE_LIMIT or AREA_LIMIT anywhere under src/.

4. Zero coverage is one contiguous subgraphtests

I patched the ratchet to print the list rather than trust a reconstruction. The 16 modules are not scattered — they are the app shell plus the entire mini-game runtime:

minigames/framework/  ComboTracker  ScoreManager   EntityPool
                      SpawnScheduler  DifficultyController

Every mini-game's scoring, combo multiplier, spawn timing, difficulty ramp and object pooling is untested. SpawnScheduler owns setTimeout handles in a repo that has two whole suites about timers outliving teardown. ScoreManager and ComboTracker are pure functions — roughly ten lines each to test.

The tooling already exists: _tsload.mjs ships bundleComponent with a fake React that captures effects for exactly this. Two suites use it; none of these sixteen.

5. The pins have no mechanical second opiniontests

Ten assertions pin a count. Generating one room moved four of them at once — which is the failure mode: a structural change presents as four unrelated numeric failures, and the cheapest response is to update four numbers.

The ratchet designed the protection and then didn't use it. coverage-baseline.json has "justifications": [], while six ad-hoc _note keys — which the script never reads, requires or validates — carry the entire real trail including the largest move.

Two further properties worth knowing: the ratchet compares set sizes, not sets, so a module silently losing coverage while another gains it passes green; and "observed" counts modules that were loaded, not executed, so the 487 is partly a bundling figure. The honest number is the one it prints and refuses to gate on: 43.2% of lines.

The first two are the exact bug two other files carry written gravestones for — "a child who walks out to the beach hears a train from a playroom that no longer exists". The fix shipped; these two call sites were missed.

FLOOR_OBSTACLES is the same pattern playroom/layout.ts has a 40-line postmortem about. I spot-checked three entries against their builders — toyBall (0.8, -4.5), spinningTop (3.0, -2.0), beanbag (-4.5, -3.5) — all three exact. Two floor props are already missing from the list.

7. Framework surface nobody usesreuse

IdleAnimator.loop() has zero call sites anywhere. Its own docblock names its motivating example as "a hopping chick, a breathing owl" — and hoppingChick.ts builds a gsap.timeline({repeat:-1}) by hand and passes it to register() instead. The abstraction's stated reason to exist bypasses it.

More broadly, register() ("adopt my raw tween") has 21 call sites against 19 for the five named verbs combined. The vocabulary is losing to the escape hatch.

Same shape in the toybox spec: interaction is set by zero specs, scale is 0.75 in all six, and 'heart' is a declared emblem kind no scene uses. Worth deleting rather than documenting.

8. One test reproduces the defect its sibling was written to documenttests

particle-engine.test.mjs reimplements sampleDirection in five lines and then proves properties of its own copy across 220,000 samples. The real function has a step the copy omits:

scratchDir.set(sinPhi * cos(theta), cosPhi, sinPhi * sin(theta));
const q = rotationFor(preset);
if (q) scratchDir.applyQuaternion(q);   <-- not in the test's copy

A wrong axis, a non-normalised quaternion, or a preset aiming its cone into the emitter is invisible to every one of those samples. This is precisely what idleAnimator.test.mjs's header was written about after the same thing happened there — and that header says the excuse ("needs a DOM") is false. It is false here too: engine.ts contains no DOM reference; the canvas dependency is one lazy hop away in texture.ts.

What I'd do next, in order

  1. Make the room guards sweep the catalog Four hardcoded lists become one derived list. This is the highest-leverage change in the audit: it converts every future room from unguarded to guarded, and it will immediately fail on the template's unnamed walls — which is the point.
  2. Give the framing predicate a home in src/ One exported function taking its thresholds as arguments; eleven copies become eleven call sites. It also brings the constants into src/, which is what makes noCopiedConstants able to see them at all.
  3. Fix the four bypasses Two are two-line fixes for a bug the repo has already written the postmortem for. FLOOR_OBSTACLES should be derived from the built scene the way perches already are.
  4. Test the mini-game runtime Start with the five pure modules — ScoreManager, ComboTracker, DifficultyController, EntityPool, SpawnScheduler. Every game's score passes through untested arithmetic. Roughly a day, and it closes two thirds of the zero-coverage set.
  5. Repair the generators Azimuth, audio and camera constraints are three literals. Worth doing after (1), so the fixed template is verified by a guard rather than by hand.
  6. Drive particle-engine and little-shark-aim instead of grepping them Both have measured, well-reasoned headers enforced by regexes over source text. The functions they are about are never called.
What I did not verify. Four surveyed claims I have left unconfirmed rather than repeat: the unused knobs on interactionController (silent, supportsDrag, setPaused, setProximityRadiusPx), the raw-material counts inside the mini-games, whether living-room was generator-produced, and the behavioural equivalence of the three coexisting teardown mechanisms.

I also corrected one survey claim: the owl was reported as scheduling nine bare setTimeout calls that would hang headless suites. It does not — setTimeout appears only in entities/owl/disposer.ts, twice, and disposeAll() clears every timer. That module is a 62-line reimplementation of DisposalScope, which is worth collapsing, but it is not a leak.
